Medical Device Design @ The Measure Lab

Deep-tech medical research — a new biosensor, a new diagnostic, a new therapy — rarely fails on the science. It fails on the platform underneath it: the measurement, the prototyping, the validation that turns a lab result into something a clinician can actually pick up. We build that layer, and pair it with a repeatable design methodology to move medical products from clinical problem to validated prototype: new requirements for existing devices, redesigns, and entirely new products for hospitals, clinics and beyond.

The name is literal, not a metaphor. Before medical devices, this was optical test-and-measurement — co-founding Southern Photonics (2002) and, later, Medimundi. Measurement built those products; measurement is what proves a medical device design actually works.

01 — Working method

Five steps, one room

Every device that comes through the lab moves through the same sequence — from a clinician's problem statement to data that goes back out into publications, patents and further trials.

01

Problem statement

  • Identify the clinical need the device must address
  • Generate initial ideas and potential solutions
  • Outline the design process
02

Component fabrication

  • Employ CAD, FEA & CFD to define geometry and behaviour
  • Manufacture parts with the appropriate technique
  • 3D-print for rapid functional proof-of-concept
03

Clean assembly

Components are brought together to form the complete device, with care taken to ensure proper fit and cleanliness at every joint.

04

Testing & validation

  • Device testing
  • Microscopic inspection
  • Data collection
  • Evaluation of performance
05

Results & outcomes

Findings go back out of the room: validated prototypes for further studies, and data that feeds publications and patent filings.

02 — Focus

Medical products, devices & diagnostics

Medical device design is an enormous space. The lab keeps a deliberately tight scope: three ways of engaging with an existing or future product, built on a research position formed over eight years of hospital-facing prototyping work.

MODE / 01

New requirements for an existing product

Working with clinicians and hospital staff to define what an already-marketed device should be able to do next.

MODE / 02

New designs for existing products

Reusable, steerable or otherwise re-engineered versions of devices already in clinical use.

MODE / 03

Entirely new products

Novel diagnostics and devices built from a clinical need with no existing commercial equivalent.

Where the lab sits today

  • Routine 3D-printing of medical devices for functional proof-of-concept
  • Keeping patients, healthcare providers and biomedical researchers in the development loop
  • Validation of pre-production devices before they leave the lab
  • Design work aimed at extending the lifetime of medical devices

PROJECT 01

Microneedle sensor for immunosuppressants

A wearable, minimally-invasive sensor to monitor immunosuppressant levels in transplant patients — built on a 3D-printed solid microneedle array. It is the project that most exposed the gap between a working bench prototype and a device that can legally touch a patient.

The photopolymer resin used for the printed microneedle array turned out not to be biocompatible — unsuitable for any human study. An IOF proof-of-concept application to bridge that gap was turned down, on the basis that a porcine model wasn't considered close enough to human physiology to justify the investment. Rather than stall, the platform was proven out on a living organism that needs neither ethical approval nor pre-clinical trials: a houseplant.

Parrilla, M., Steijlen, A., Kerremans, R., Jacobs, J., den Haan, L., De Vreese, J., Van Noten Géron, Y., Clerx, P., Watts, R. & De Wael, K. "Wearable platform based on 3D-printed solid microneedle potentiometric pH sensor for plant monitoring." Chemical Engineering Journal 500 (2024): 157254.

PROJECT 02

Wearable biosensors with A-PECS

Alongside the microneedle work, the lab builds the wearable hardware for a run of potentiometric and electrochemical sensors developed with the A-PECS electrochemistry group (Prof. Karolien De Wael): a wristband phenylalanine sensor for PKU patients, and the solid-microneedle pH platform that underpins the immunosuppressant work above.

The lab's contribution sits at the mechanical end — 3D-printed enclosures, wearable form factors, and adhesion/penetration/ingress testing on porcine and human skin before anything is trusted against a person.

06 — How we collaborate

Complementary collaboration

The specialty, named plainly: we don't compete with a clinical or scientific specialist's deep-tech — we build the measurement and prototyping layer underneath it, so their work can actually reach a market. Thirty-two masters theses and collaborations with more than a dozen senior professors across UAntwerpen/UZA's five 'Health' sub-domains are the evidence.

01

Joint master's thesis

A company, hospital department or research group brings a real problem; a Product Development student spends a year on it, co-supervised from both sides.

Partners: UZA, Helicus, A-PECS, Bosch, ABB, Voxdale, OxyPoint, INBO — 32 projects since 2019
02

Applied R&D & proof-of-concept

Funded projects that carry an idea from lab bench toward something a company or hospital can actually adopt — IOF, VLAIO, BOF and FWO all fund different stages of that path.

Current: NeoVOC, SafeTrawl I & II, TEAMBIO, INFLUXO, REmedi
03

Cross-institution & cross-discipline research

Work that only happens by pairing measurement and prototyping with expertise the lab doesn't have in-house — electrochemistry, biomedical imaging, ecology, robotics. More than a dozen senior professors across the five 'Health' sub-domains have brought instrumentation and co-development tracks here over the years.

With: Els Du Bois (REuse Lab) & Karolien De Wael (A-PECS) on Nature4Nature · Tom Breugelmans on electrochemistry · Winnok De Vos & Jan Sijbers on biomedical imaging · KU Leuven Surgical Robotics
04

Spin-off & start-up formation

When a proof-of-concept clears the bar, the lab's own track record is that it doesn't stop at the paper: co-founder of Southern Photonics (2002), whose daughter company Quantifi Photonics secured Series A, B and C venture funding and was acquired by NASDAQ-listed Teradyne for €125M in March 2025; and Medimundi NV (2020), which took FFP2/FFP3 respirator design from patent to production line during COVID-19.

Evidence of the appetite: patents as the output that matters, not just the paper — the Medimundi patents alone unlocked multi-million-dollar investment in production.
07 — Infrastructure

The room itself

50 m² on the Stadscampus, built for the full loop from CAD file to sterilised, tested device. Most problems that arrive from industry or a masters thesis get solved the same way: build a testopstelling — a purpose-built rig — that isolates the one design parameter in question, then measure until the answer is unambiguous. Plus access to the wider Antwerp Design Factory for anything the lab itself can't handle.
